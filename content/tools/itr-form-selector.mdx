# ITR Form Selector: A Complete Explainer

Selecting the correct Income Tax Return (ITR) form is the fundamental first step of filing your taxes in India. Choosing the wrong form can result in your return being processed as defective, leading to notices and delays in refunds. This guide will walk you through how the selection works, examples, and when you should seek professional help.

## How this selection works (with the logic written out)

The logic behind selecting an ITR form depends primarily on the type of income you earn and your total income limit. Here is the step-by-step logic our selector uses:

1. **ITR-1 (Sahaj)**: 
   - This form is selected if you are a Resident Individual (not applicable to HUFs or Non-Residents).
   - Your total income must be up to ₹50 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY -->.
   - Income sources can include Salary/Pension, One House Property, and Other Sources (like Interest, Dividends, Agricultural Income up to ₹5,000 <!-- VERIFY -->).
   - *Exclusions*: It is not applicable if you have capital gains, business/profession income, income from more than one house property, foreign assets/income, or if you are a director in a company or hold unlisted equity shares.

2. **ITR-2**:
   - Selected for Individuals and HUFs not having income from Business or Profession under any proprietorship.
   - It applies if your income exceeds ₹50 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY -->.
   - Applicable if you have Capital Gains (short-term or long-term from property, shares, etc.).
   - Used when you have income from More than One House Property.
   - Mandatory if you hold Foreign Assets or have Foreign Income.
   - Required if you are a Director in a company or hold Unlisted Equity Shares.

3. **ITR-3**:
   - This is the most comprehensive form for Individuals and HUFs.
   - Selected if you have income under the head "Profits and gains of business or profession" (this includes regular business, proprietary business, and professional income like freelancing).
   - It covers all income sources included in ITR-1 and ITR-2, plus business income.
   - Also used by partners of a firm if they have income from the firm like salary, interest, bonus, commission, or remuneration.

4. **ITR-4 (Sugam)**:
   - For Resident Individuals, HUFs, and Firms (other than LLP) having total income up to ₹50 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY -->.
   - Applicable if you have income from Business or Profession which is computed under the presumptive taxation scheme (sections 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE).
   - Under Section 44ADA, the gross receipts from the profession should not exceed ₹50 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY --> (or ₹75 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY --> if cash receipts are less than 5% <!-- VERIFY -->).
   - Under Section 44AD, business turnover should not exceed ₹2 Crores <!-- VERIFY --> (or ₹3 Crores <!-- VERIFY --> if cash receipts are less than 5% <!-- VERIFY -->).

## A worked example (with real business/income sources)

Let’s look at a realistic scenario for a modern taxpayer, Ravi.

**Ravi's Income Sources for the Financial Year:**
- **Salary:** ₹12,000,000 <!-- VERIFY --> from his full-time job as a software engineer.
- **Freelance Income:** ₹15,00,000 <!-- VERIFY --> from side gigs (designing websites). He wants to opt for presumptive taxation.
- **Capital Gains:** He sold some mutual funds resulting in a Short Term Capital Gain of ₹50,000 <!-- VERIFY -->.
- **Interest Income:** ₹20,000 <!-- VERIFY --> from a savings account.

**Logic Application:**
- Ravi has a salary (which points to ITR-1 or ITR-2), but he also has freelance income, which falls under business/profession.
- Freelance income can be filed under presumptive taxation (ITR-4) if total income is under ₹50 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY -->.
- However, Ravi's total income is ₹12,000,000 (Salary) + ₹15,00,000 (Freelance) + ₹50,000 (Capital Gains) + ₹20,000 (Interest) = ₹13,570,000 <!-- VERIFY -->. This exceeds ₹50 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY -->.
- Additionally, he has Capital Gains, which immediately disqualifies him from ITR-1 and ITR-4.
- Because he has Business/Profession income (freelance) and Capital Gains, he cannot use ITR-2 either.
- **Result:** Ravi must file **ITR-3**. The selector accurately identifies that business income combined with capital gains requires the most comprehensive form, ITR-3.

## Edge cases handled and not handled

Our selector handles a majority of standard and mixed-income scenarios gracefully. However, there are complexities in the Indian tax system.

**Edge Cases Handled:**
- **Presumptive Taxation limits:** Differentiating between standard business income (ITR-3) and presumptive business income (ITR-4) based on the ₹50 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY --> / ₹2 Crores <!-- VERIFY --> limits.
- **Director / Unlisted Shares:** Catching the rule that even with a simple salary under ₹50 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY -->, holding unlisted shares immediately forces an ITR-2 or ITR-3.
- **Agricultural Income:** Differentiating between minor agricultural income (up to ₹5,000 <!-- VERIFY --> allowed in ITR-1) and major agricultural income.

**Edge Cases NOT Handled (Requires specific attention):**
- **Crypto / VDA (Virtual Digital Assets) complexities:** While standard crypto gains are taxed at 30% <!-- VERIFY -->, whether they classify as Capital Gains or Business Income depends on transaction frequency and intent. The selector assumes Capital Gains by default for non-business users.
- **Brought Forward Losses:** If you only have salary this year but need to carry forward a business loss from a previous year, you must use ITR-3, even if this year's income suggests ITR-1. The basic selector only looks at current year income heads.
- **Non-Resident nuances:** The selector defaults to standard NRI rules (usually ITR-2 or ITR-3), but DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement) claims require specific schedules not factored into simple form selection.

## When you actually need a professional

While an ITR form selector is a fantastic starting point, it is not a substitute for a Chartered Accountant (CA) or a tax professional, especially in complex scenarios. Honest advice: a simple selector is not enough when your financial life gets complicated. You should seek a professional if:

1. **Complex Capital Gains:** Calculating capital gains on real estate involves indexation, checking circle rates (Section 50C), and claiming exemptions (Section 54, 54F). Even for stocks, grandfathering rules for long-term gains pre-2018 can be confusing.
2. **Foreign Assets and Income (Schedule FA):** If you hold RSU/ESOPs of foreign parent companies (like Google, Amazon) or have foreign bank accounts, you must fill out Schedule FA. The reporting requirements are incredibly strict, and failure to report can attract severe penalties under the Black Money Act (up to ₹10 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY --> per year).
3. **Clubbing of Income:** If you have invested in the name of your minor child or spouse and the income generated needs to be clubbed with your income, calculating the exact liability and exemptions (like the ₹1,500 <!-- VERIFY --> per child exemption) requires care.
4. **Unlisted Shares:** If you have bought or sold unlisted shares, you need to report the Fair Market Value (FMV) as computed by a registered valuer.
5. **Business Restructuring:** If you are dissolving a partnership, merging proprietorships, or handling complex brought forward losses from multiple years, professional help is mandatory to ensure continuity and avoid defective returns.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**1. Can I use ITR-1 if I earned ₹1 Lakh <!-- VERIFY --> from intraday stock trading?**
No. Intraday stock trading is considered speculative business income. You must file ITR-3 to report business income, regardless of your other income sources.

**2. I have a salary of ₹60 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY --> and no other income. Can I file ITR-1?**
No. Since your total income exceeds ₹50 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY -->, you are not eligible for ITR-1. You must file ITR-2.

**3. I am an NRI with only interest income from an NRO account in India amounting to ₹40,000 <!-- VERIFY -->. Which form should I use?**
NRIs cannot use ITR-1, even if the income is below ₹50 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY -->. You must file ITR-2 to report your Indian income.

**4. I have freelance income and want to use ITR-4. Do I need to maintain books of accounts?**
If you opt for the presumptive taxation scheme under section 44ADA (for professionals) and declare 50% <!-- VERIFY --> or more of your gross receipts as income, you are generally not required to maintain detailed books of accounts.

**5. I sold my house and reinvested the money in another house to claim Section 54 exemption. Which ITR form?**
You have generated Capital Gains (even if they are fully exempted). Therefore, you must file ITR-2 to declare the sale consideration, capital gains, and the exemption claimed under Section 54.

**6. I won ₹10,000 <!-- VERIFY --> in a fantasy cricket app. Does this change my ITR form?**
Winnings from lotteries, online games, etc., are taxed under "Income from Other Sources" at a flat rate of 30% <!-- VERIFY -->. While you can technically report this in ITR-1, if you have any business income related to gaming, it pushes you to ITR-3. If it's pure casual winnings and total income is under ₹50 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY -->, ITR-1 is usually acceptable, but ITR-2 provides clearer reporting schedules for special rate incomes.
