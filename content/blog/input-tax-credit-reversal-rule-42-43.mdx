# Input Tax Credit reversal under Rule 42/43, explained without the jargon

If you use goods or services for both business and non-business purposes, or for making both taxable and exempt supplies, you cannot claim the entire Input Tax Credit (ITC). <Verify>Rule 42</Verify> outlines the reversal calculation for inputs and input services, while <Verify>Rule 43</Verify> governs the reversal for capital goods. You must reverse the proportionate ITC attributable to exempt or non-business purposes.

## Why is there a need to reverse Input Tax Credit?

Input Tax Credit is the backbone of the Goods and Services Tax (GST) system. It ensures that tax is only levied on the value addition at each stage of the supply chain, preventing the cascading effect of taxes. However, the fundamental principle of claiming ITC is that it can only be availed if the inward supplies (purchases) are used in the course or furtherance of business to make taxable supplies.

When a business uses its purchases entirely to make taxable products or services, claiming the credit is straightforward. You buy raw materials, you pay GST, and you claim that GST as credit against your outward tax liability. But reality is rarely this simple.

Many businesses operate in a mixed environment. A company might produce both taxable goods (like shoes) and exempt goods (like certain agricultural produce). Or, a business owner might use a company laptop for personal work on weekends. In these scenarios, the GST law dictates that you cannot claim the full ITC on the raw materials or the laptop.

This is where the concept of ITC reversal comes in. You are required to calculate the portion of the input tax that corresponds to the exempt supplies or non-business use and reverse it—meaning you pay it back to the government or reduce it from your available credit ledger. <Verify>Rule 42</Verify> and <Verify>Rule 43</Verify> of the Central Goods and Services Tax (<Verify>CGST</Verify>) Rules, <Verify>2017</Verify>, provide the exact mathematical formulas and procedures for doing this correctly.

## How does Rule 42 work for inputs and input services?

<Verify>Rule 42</Verify> of the CGST Rules specifically deals with the apportionment of ITC and its reversal in respect of inputs and input services. Inputs refer to the raw materials or consumables used in your business, while input services refer to the services you procure, such as advertising, rent, or legal consultations.

The rule operates on a simple logic: first, identify all the tax you paid on inputs and services. Then, systematically filter out the portions that are not eligible for credit. What remains is your eligible common credit. You then mathematically divide this common credit based on the ratio of your exempt turnover to your total turnover, and reverse the portion attributed to the exempt turnover.

The rule provides a step-by-step mechanism to ensure that the calculation is objective and uniform across all businesses. While the formulas might look daunting at first glance, breaking them down into individual steps makes them quite manageable.

## What is the step-by-step calculation under Rule 42?

To accurately calculate the reversal under <Verify>Rule 42</Verify>, you must follow a sequential process for every tax period (typically every month). Here is the detailed breakdown:

**Step 1: Determine Total ITC (T)**
Calculate the total input tax on inputs and input services for the tax period. Let's call this amount "T".

**Step 2: Identify Exclusively Ineligible ITC**
From the total ITC (T), you need to subtract the ITC that you know for sure is not eligible. This is broken down into three variables:
- **T1**: ITC on inputs/services used exclusively for non-business purposes.
- **T2**: ITC on inputs/services used exclusively for making exempt supplies.
- **T3**: ITC that is blocked under <Verify>Section 17(5)</Verify> of the CGST Act (e.g., food and beverages, club memberships, motor vehicles with certain exceptions).

**Step 3: Calculate ITC Credited to Electronic Credit Ledger (C1)**
Subtract T1, T2, and T3 from the total ITC (T).
*Formula: C1 = T - (T1 + T2 + T3)*
This amount (C1) is credited to your Electronic Credit Ledger.

**Step 4: Identify Exclusively Eligible ITC (T4)**
Identify the ITC on inputs/services used exclusively for making taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies). Let's call this "T4".

**Step 5: Calculate Common Credit (C2)**
Subtract the exclusively eligible ITC (T4) from the credit in the ledger (C1).
*Formula: C2 = C1 - T4*
This common credit (C2) is the amount that needs to be apportioned because it represents inputs/services used for both taxable and exempt/non-business purposes.

**Step 6: Calculate Reversal for Exempt Supplies (D1)**
Apportion the common credit (C2) towards exempt supplies based on turnover.
*Formula: D1 = (Exempt Turnover / Total Turnover) × C2*
This is the amount of credit you must reverse for making exempt supplies.

**Step 7: Calculate Reversal for Non-Business Use (D2)**
If you cannot pinpoint the exact non-business usage, <Verify>Rule 42</Verify> mandates a flat reversal.
*Formula: D2 = <Verify>5%</Verify> of C2*
This is the amount reversed for deemed non-business use.

**Step 8: Final Eligible Common Credit (C3)**
The remaining common credit after these reversals is your truly eligible common credit.
*Formula: C3 = C2 - (D1 + D2)*

You must report these reversals (D1 and D2) in your <Verify>GSTR-3B</Verify> returns and add them to your output tax liability.

## How does Rule 43 govern capital goods?

While <Verify>Rule 42</Verify> handles raw materials and services, <Verify>Rule 43</Verify> of the CGST Rules dictates how to reverse ITC on capital goods. Capital goods are assets like machinery, equipment, computers, or furniture that are capitalized in your books of accounts and used in the business over a long period.

Because capital goods have a long lifespan, GST law presumes their useful life to be <Verify>60 months</Verify> (5 years) for the purpose of ITC calculation. You cannot simply claim the ITC once and forget about it if the usage of that machine changes from purely taxable to partially exempt over those five years.

<Verify>Rule 43</Verify> requires you to calculate the monthly ITC attributable to capital goods and then apportion that monthly credit between taxable and exempt supplies every single month for <Verify>60 months</Verify>.

## What are the exact steps for Rule 43 reversal?

The mechanism for <Verify>Rule 43</Verify> is slightly more complex due to the amortized nature of the credit over the <Verify>60-month</Verify> period. Here is how it is structured:

**Step 1: Segregate Capital Goods by Use**
Identify the capital goods and categorize their ITC:
- **Category A**: Used exclusively for non-business purposes or exempt supplies. (No ITC allowed).
- **Category B**: Used exclusively for taxable supplies (including zero-rated). (Full ITC allowed immediately).
- **Category C**: Used commonly for both taxable and exempt/non-business purposes. Let's call the ITC on these "A".

If a capital good moves from Category A or B into Category C, you must bring its ITC into the common pool. If it moves from Category A to C, you deduct <Verify>5%</Verify> per quarter from the date of invoice and add the rest to the common pool "A".

**Step 2: Calculate Useful Life Monthly Credit (Tm)**
For common capital goods (Category C), the total ITC "A" must be divided by <Verify>60</Verify> to find the monthly credit.
*Formula: Tm = A / <Verify>60</Verify>*

**Step 3: Calculate Total Common Monthly Credit (Tr)**
Sum up the 'Tm' values for all capital goods that fall into the common category to get the total common credit for the month, denoted as "Tr".

**Step 4: Apportion Towards Exempt Supplies (Te)**
Now, apportion the total common monthly credit (Tr) towards exempt supplies based on the turnover ratio of the current tax period.
*Formula: Te = (Exempt Turnover / Total Turnover) × Tr*

This amount, Te, must be added to your output tax liability along with applicable interest. This calculation must be repeated every month for <Verify>60 months</Verify> from the date of invoice of the capital goods.

## How do these rules impact different sectors?

The impact of Rule 42 and Rule 43 varies significantly across different industries. Here is a structured look at how it affects common sectors:

| Sector | Nature of Mixed Supplies | Impact of Rule 42 / 43 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Real Estate & Construction** | Commercial units (taxable) vs. Residential affordable units (exempt/different rates) | Very high impact. Extensive common credit apportionment required for cement, steel, architect fees, and construction machinery. |
| **Healthcare** | Room rent below <Verify>Rs. 5,000</Verify> and treatments (exempt) vs. Pharmacy sales & cosmetic surgery (taxable) | High impact. Common inputs like housekeeping, security, and medical equipment must be meticulously apportioned. |
| **Education** | Core curriculum education (exempt) vs. Extracurricular classes, uniform sales, canteen (taxable) | Moderate to High. Need to segregate credits for building maintenance, IT infrastructure, and marketing services. |
| **IT & Software Services** | Domestic software sales (taxable) vs. Software exports (zero-rated, acts as taxable) | Low impact. Since exports are zero-rated, most ITC is fully eligible. Reversals only apply if there are specific exempt non-IT services. |

## What happens at the end of the financial year?

The calculations done every month under Rule 42 for inputs and input services are provisional. At the end of the financial year, you must recalculate the entire Rule 42 reversal using the annual exempt turnover and annual total turnover for the whole year.

This annual calculation must be completed before filing the GST return for the month of September following the end of the financial year, or before filing the annual return, whichever is earlier (though recent amendments have extended this to November <Verify>30th</Verify> for some compliances).

If the annual calculation shows you reversed *less* during the year than you should have, you must reverse the shortfall and pay interest at <Verify>18%</Verify> per annum from the 1st of April of the succeeding year until the date of payment.

If the annual calculation shows you reversed *more* than required, you can claim the excess back as credit in your electronic credit ledger.

This annual reconciliation is a critical part of year-end finalization and is closely scrutinized during the preparation of [GSTR 9 and 9C Reconciliations](/blog/gstr-9-9c-filing-reconciliations). Maintaining a strict [Business Compliance Calendar](/blog/business-compliance-calendar-gstin) is vital to ensure you do not miss this true-up window.

## What are the penalties for non-compliance?

Failing to reverse ITC when required, or miscalculating the reversal, is viewed by the tax authorities as claiming ineligible input tax credit.

Under <Verify>Section 73</Verify> of the CGST Act (for cases involving no fraud or willful misstatement), the penalty is typically <Verify>10%</Verify> of the tax amount or <Verify>Rs. 10,000</Verify>, whichever is higher, plus the mandatory payment of the principal amount and interest at <Verify>18%</Verify> per annum.

Under <Verify>Section 74</Verify> (for cases involving fraud, suppression of facts, or willful misstatement with intent to evade tax), the penalty can go up to <Verify>100%</Verify> of the tax evaded, along with the principal and <Verify>18%</Verify> interest.

Furthermore, discrepancies between the ITC claimed and the ITC eligible based on your turnover ratios are easily flagged by the GST Network's automated scrutiny systems. Such red flags inevitably lead to departmental notices, audits, and extended litigation.

## How can businesses automate and manage this complexity?

Manual calculations of Rule 42 and 43 in spreadsheets are prone to human error, especially as transaction volumes grow. Businesses should adopt robust ERP systems or specialized GST compliance software.

A good system will allow you to tag every purchase invoice at the entry stage as 'eligible', 'ineligible', or 'common'. It should automatically pull turnover data from your sales register, apply the formulas for Rule 42, track the <Verify>60-month</Verify> lifecycle for capital goods under Rule 43, and generate the exact figures to be populated in Table 4 of your <Verify>GSTR-3B</Verify>.

Regular training for the accounting team and periodic internal audits are also essential to ensure that the initial tagging of invoices—the foundation of the whole calculation—is done correctly.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. Is Rule 43 applicable if I sell a capital asset before <Verify>60 months</Verify>?**
Yes. If you sell a capital asset before its useful life of <Verify>60 months</Verify> is over, you must pay an amount equal to the ITC taken on the asset reduced by <Verify>5%</Verify> per quarter of a year from the invoice date, or the tax on the transaction value of the sale, whichever is higher, as per <Verify>Section 18(6)</Verify> of the CGST Act.

**2. Does bank interest count towards 'exempt turnover' for Rule 42/43?**
No. By way of a specific explanation inserted in the CGST Rules, the value of exempt supply for the purpose of apportionment of ITC shall not include the value of supply of services by way of extending deposits, loans, or advances in so far as the consideration is represented by way of interest or discount. (Except for a banking company or financial institution).

**3. Do I need to reverse ITC for providing free samples or gifts?**
Yes. Goods given as free samples or gifts are blocked from ITC under <Verify>Section 17(5)(h)</Verify>. This is not an apportionment issue under Rule 42; the entire ITC on such goods must be reversed outright.

**4. How do I calculate total turnover for the formula?**
Total turnover in the State (or Union territory) includes all taxable supplies, exempt supplies, zero-rated supplies, and non-GST supplies made from that specific registration, but excludes inward supplies on which tax is payable under reverse charge, and central tax, state tax, union territory tax, integrated tax, and cess.

**5. Can I skip the monthly calculation and just do it annually?**
No. The law requires you to calculate and reverse the proportionate ITC every tax period (monthly or quarterly depending on your filing status). The annual calculation is merely a true-up exercise to correct any discrepancies that occurred due to fluctuating monthly turnover ratios.
