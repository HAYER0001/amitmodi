# Filing an appeal before the CIT(A): timelines, fees and what to prepare

Filing an appeal before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), or CIT(A), requires submitting Form No. 35 electronically within <Verify>30 days</Verify> of receiving the assessment order or demand notice. The appeal fee ranges from <Verify>₹250</Verify> to <Verify>₹1,000</Verify>, depending on the assessed income. Key preparations include drafting a Statement of Facts and the Grounds of Appeal.

Dealing with an adverse income tax assessment order can be an incredibly stressful experience for any taxpayer. However, the Income Tax Act of 1961 provides a robust mechanism for addressing grievances. The first appellate authority in this hierarchy is the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals). This article provides an in-depth look into the entire process of filing an appeal before the CIT(A), the stringent timelines you must adhere to, the applicable fees, and exactly what you need to prepare to ensure your appeal has the highest chance of success. 

If you are dealing with other types of notices, you might also want to read our guides on [understanding a Section 148 reassessment notice](/blog/section-148-reassessment-notice) and [what happens after a GST notice](/blog/what-happens-after-gst-notice).

## What exactly is an appeal to the CIT(A) and when should you file one?

When an Income Tax Officer (Assessing Officer or AO) passes an order that you believe is incorrect, unjust, or legally flawed, you have the right to challenge it. The appeal to the CIT(A) is your first formal opportunity to have an independent, higher-ranking tax official review the AO's decision. 

You should consider filing an appeal in various scenarios. The most common situation is when the AO has made additions to your declared income, disallowing certain expenses, exemptions, or deductions that you claimed in your [income tax returns](/services/income-tax-returns). 

Another frequent reason for appeal is the levy of a penalty under various sections, such as <Verify>Section 270A</Verify> for under-reporting or misreporting of income. 

Furthermore, you might want to appeal if the AO has rejected your application for rectification under <Verify>Section 154</Verify>, or if there are disputes regarding the computation of your tax liability, the status under which you are assessed, or the set-off of losses. 

It is crucial to understand that the CIT(A) has the power to confirm, reduce, enhance, or annul the assessment. Therefore, while filing an appeal is a right, it is a legal process that requires meticulous preparation and a solid foundation in tax jurisprudence.

## What are the strict timelines for filing an appeal before the CIT(A)?

The Income Tax Act mandates very specific and rigid timelines for filing an appeal. Missing these deadlines can jeopardize your entire case, potentially leading to the dismissal of your appeal before it is even heard on its merits.

Under <Verify>Section 249(2)</Verify> of the Income Tax Act, you must file the appeal within <Verify>30 days</Verify> from the date of the receipt of the order being appealed against, or the notice of demand relating to the penalty. 

The <Verify>30 days</Verify> period begins on the day after the order or notice is served upon you. It is paramount to retain the envelope, email, or screenshot of the portal demonstrating the exact date of receipt. 

### Can the CIT(A) condone a delay in filing the appeal?

Yes, the CIT(A) possesses the discretionary power to admit an appeal filed after the expiry of the <Verify>30 days</Verify> period. However, this is not an automatic right. You must file a formal application for condonation of delay along with the appeal. 

In this application, you must demonstrate "sufficient cause" for the delay. Sufficient cause implies reasons beyond your control, such as a severe medical emergency, natural calamities, or postal delays (if applicable, though most orders are now digital). 

The burden of proof lies entirely on the taxpayer to justify every single day of delay. It is strongly advised not to rely on condonation and to make every effort to file within the statutory <Verify>30 days</Verify>.

## What are the appeal fees for filing with the CIT(A)?

Filing an appeal requires the payment of an appeal fee under <Verify>Section 249(1)</Verify>. The fee structure is progressive, based on the total income computed by the Assessing Officer in the case to which the appeal relates. 

This fee must be paid before filing Form 35, as the challan details (BSR Code, Date of Deposit, and Challan Serial Number) are mandatory fields in the appeal form.

Here is the structured breakdown of the appeal fees:

| Total Assessed Income (as computed by AO) | Appeal Fee Applicable |
| :--- | :--- |
| Less than or equal to <Verify>₹1,00,000</Verify> | <Verify>₹250</Verify> |
| More than <Verify>₹1,00,000</Verify> but less than <Verify>₹2,00,000</Verify> | <Verify>₹500</Verify> |
| More than <Verify>₹2,00,000</Verify> | <Verify>₹1,000</Verify> |
| Any other subject matter (e.g., TDS defaults, penalty where income is not assessed) | <Verify>₹250</Verify> |

You can pay this fee online through the e-Filing portal using the e-Pay Tax functionality. Select Minor Head "300" (Self Assessment Tax/Other Receipts) and Major Head "021" (Income Tax - Other than Companies) or "020" (Corporation Tax) as applicable to your taxpayer status. It is advisable to specify that the payment is for the "Appeal Fee" in the payment description if such an option is available, to avoid any misallocation.

## What documents and information must you prepare for Form 35?

Filing an appeal is no longer a physical, paper-based process. It is done entirely online through Form No. 35 on the Income Tax e-Filing portal. However, before you even open the portal, you must have your documentation meticulously prepared.

The preparation phase is arguably the most critical part of the entire appeal process. A poorly drafted appeal will severely undermine your chances of success, no matter how strong your underlying case might be. 

Here is what you need to prepare:

### 1. The Statement of Facts
The Statement of Facts (SoF) is a chronological narrative of the events that led to the assessment order and the subsequent appeal. It is the foundation upon which your Grounds of Appeal rest.

The SoF must be purely factual. It should not contain legal arguments, citations of case laws, or emotional appeals. It should simply state who you are, the nature of your business, what return you filed, when the scrutiny began, what information was requested, what was provided, and what the AO eventually concluded. 

A well-drafted Statement of Facts helps the CIT(A) understand the background of the case instantly. It should be concise, numbered paragraph by paragraph, and free of any aggressive language against the Assessing Officer.

### 2. The Grounds of Appeal
The Grounds of Appeal are the specific legal and factual points on which you are challenging the AO's order. They are the heart of your appeal.

Each ground should address a specific addition, disallowance, or error made by the AO. Grounds should be drafted clearly, concisely, and without argumentative language. They are typically structured to state that the AO erred in law and/or on facts in making a particular addition. 

For example, a ground might read: "The Ld. Assessing Officer erred in law and on facts in disallowing business promotion expenses of <Verify>₹5,00,000</Verify> under <Verify>Section 37(1)</Verify> of the Act." 

You must prepare a separate ground for each distinct issue. The CIT(A) generally restricts the hearing to the grounds raised in Form 35, although additional grounds can sometimes be admitted later if they are purely legal and do not require fresh factual investigation.

### 3. Essential Attachments
You must prepare PDF copies of several essential documents to attach with your Form 35:
- The completely completely Assessment Order against which you are appealing.
- The Notice of Demand issued under <Verify>Section 156</Verify>.
- The challan receipt proving the payment of the appeal fee.
- If the appeal is filed late, a detailed application for condonation of delay along with supporting evidence (e.g., medical certificates).

Ensure that all scanned documents are legible and comply with the file size limits specified on the e-Filing portal (usually <Verify>5 MB</Verify> per attachment).

## What is the requirement regarding the payment of undisputed tax?

Under <Verify>Section 249(4)</Verify> of the Income Tax Act, an appeal shall not be admitted by the CIT(A) unless the taxpayer has paid the undisputed tax liability before filing the appeal. 

If you have filed a return of income, you must have paid the tax due on the income returned by you. If you did not file a return of income, you must have paid an amount equal to the advance tax that was payable by you.

In extremely rare cases, the CIT(A) may, for good and sufficient reasons recorded in writing, exempt the taxpayer from paying the advance tax (where no return was filed). However, the tax on the admitted/returned income must absolutely be paid. Failing to do so renders the appeal defective and liable to be dismissed in limine (at the threshold).

It is also highly recommended to pay <Verify>20%</Verify> of the disputed tax demand to obtain a stay of demand from the Assessing Officer while the appeal is pending, as per the CBDT instructions. If you do not obtain a stay, the tax department can initiate coercive recovery proceedings (like bank attachment) even while your appeal is sub-judice.

## How do you draft an effective Statement of Facts and Grounds of Appeal?

Drafting these documents is an art that requires both a deep understanding of tax law and excellent communication skills. 

### Best Practices for Statement of Facts
- **Chronology is Key:** Start with the filing of the return, move to the issuance of the scrutiny notice, detail the proceedings, and end with the passing of the order.
- **Be Objective:** State facts as they are. If the AO asked for 10 invoices and you provided 8, state exactly that. Do not try to justify the missing 2 invoices in the facts section; save that for written submissions during the hearing.
- **Reference Annexures:** If referring to specific documents submitted during assessment, mention them clearly.
- **Keep it Brief:** Avoid writing a novel. Stick to the facts relevant to the disputed additions.

### Best Practices for Grounds of Appeal
- **Issue-Based Approach:** Each ground should tackle one specific addition. Do not club multiple unrelated additions into a single ground.
- **Use Clear Legal Terminology:** Employ standard legal phrasing, such as "erred in law," "without appreciating the facts," or "based on surmises and conjectures."
- **Without Prejudice:** Use the phrase "Without prejudice to Ground No. X" when raising an alternative argument. For instance, your primary ground might be that a receipt is not taxable at all. Your "without prejudice" secondary ground could be that even if it is taxable, certain expenses should be deducted from it.
- **Avoid Lengthy Arguments:** Grounds should be crisp statements of the grievance. Detailed arguments, citations of High Court or Supreme Court judgments, and elaborate calculations belong in the "Written Submissions" or "Paper Book," which you will file later during the appellate proceedings, not in the Form 35 itself.

## What is the step-by-step process to file the appeal online?

Filing Form 35 on the Income Tax portal is a structured process. Here is a general step-by-step guide:

1. **Login:** Log into your account on the Income Tax e-Filing portal using your PAN and password.
2. **Navigate:** Go to the 'e-File' menu, select 'Income Tax Forms', and then click on 'File Income Tax Forms'.
3. **Select Form:** Search for or select "Form No. 35 - Appeal to Commissioner of Appeals".
4. **Select Assessment Year:** Choose the Assessment Year for which you are filing the appeal.
5. **Fill the Form:** The form has multiple sections.
   - **Basic Information:** Your PAN, address, etc. (mostly auto-filled).
   - **Order Details:** Provide the Section under which the order was passed, the date of the order, and the DIN (Document Identification Number) of the order.
   - **Appeal Details:** Enter the details of the additions made by the AO and the disputed tax demand.
   - **Statement of Facts and Grounds of Appeal:** You have the option to either type these directly into the provided text boxes (limited character count, usually <Verify>10,000 characters</Verify>) or attach them as separate PDF documents. Attaching them as PDFs is generally preferred for formatting and length reasons.
   - **Fee Details:** Enter the BSR code, date, serial number, and amount of the appeal fee paid.
6. **Upload Attachments:** Upload the mandatory documents (Order, Demand Notice, Fee Receipt).
7. **Verification:** Verify the form. This can be done using Aadhaar OTP, EVC (Electronic Verification Code) via Net Banking, or a Digital Signature Certificate (DSC). Companies and tax audit cases generally require a DSC.
8. **Submission:** Submit the form. You will receive an acknowledgment number and a confirmation email. 

## What happens after you file the appeal? The Faceless Appeal Scheme

In recent years, the Income Tax Department has revolutionized the appellate process with the introduction of the Faceless Appeal Scheme, 2020. 

Under this scheme, appeals before the CIT(A) are handled in a jurisdiction-free, dynamic manner. When you file an appeal, it is randomly allocated to a CIT(A) anywhere in the country by the National Faceless Appeal Centre (NFAC). 

The entire process is conducted electronically. You will not know the identity or location of the CIT(A) handling your case, and there are no physical meetings or hearings. All communication is conducted through the e-Filing portal via notices and your written responses.

When your case is taken up for hearing, you will receive a notice from the NFAC asking you to submit your detailed written arguments and documentary evidence (the Paper Book). You must meticulously prepare your submissions, citing relevant case laws and providing comprehensive explanations for your grounds of appeal.

If you believe your case is highly complex and requires an oral explanation, you can request a video conferencing hearing. The Chief Commissioner or the Director General has the discretion to grant this request based on the complexity of the facts or the law involved. 

Because the process is entirely written and faceless, the quality of your written submissions is absolutely paramount. Vague or poorly structured arguments will likely result in a dismissal of the appeal.

## What are the common mistakes to avoid when filing an appeal?

Taxpayers frequently make errors that compromise their appeals. Avoid these common pitfalls:
- **Missing the Deadline:** The <Verify>30 days</Verify> deadline is strict. Do not wait until the 29th day to start preparing.
- **Failing to Pay the Appeal Fee Correctly:** Ensure you pay the exact fee based on the assessed income.
- **Not Paying Undisputed Taxes:** Your appeal will not be admitted if you haven't paid the taxes on the income you originally declared.
- **Drafting Poor Grounds of Appeal:** Vague, emotional, or excessively long grounds hurt your case. Keep them precise and legal.
- **Mixing Facts and Arguments in the SoF:** Keep the Statement of Facts purely factual. Save the arguments for later.
- **Ignoring E-Filing Notices:** Under the Faceless system, missing an electronic notice from the NFAC can lead to an ex-parte order against you. You must monitor your registered email and the e-Filing portal dashboard regularly.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. Can I file an appeal before the CIT(A) offline in paper format?**
No, physical filing of Form 35 is no longer permitted for most taxpayers. It must be filed electronically on the Income Tax e-Filing portal. Exceptions exist only in very specific, rare circumstances where electronic filing is not possible due to systemic issues.

**2. Is it mandatory to hire a Chartered Accountant or a lawyer to file the appeal?**
While not legally mandatory, it is highly advisable. The appellate process involves complex legal interpretation, drafting technical grounds, and representing the case through detailed written submissions. Professional expertise significantly increases the chances of a favorable outcome.

**3. What happens if I win the appeal before the CIT(A)?**
If the CIT(A) rules in your favor, they will pass an order directing the Assessing Officer to delete the additions or reduce the tax liability. You will then be eligible for a refund of any excess tax paid, along with interest under <Verify>Section 244A</Verify>.

**4. What if the CIT(A) rejects my appeal?**
If the CIT(A) confirms the AO's order or enhances the assessment, your next recourse is to file a second appeal before the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT). This must be done within <Verify>60 days</Verify> of receiving the CIT(A)'s order.

**5. Does filing an appeal automatically stay the recovery of the tax demand?**
No, simply filing an appeal does not stop the tax department from recovering the demand. You must file a separate application for a stay of demand with the Assessing Officer. Typically, a stay is granted upon payment of <Verify>20%</Verify> of the disputed demand, subject to the AO's discretion and CBDT guidelines.

**6. How long does the CIT(A) take to dispose of an appeal?**
There is no strict statutory time limit for the CIT(A) to pass an order. Under <Verify>Section 250(6A)</Verify>, the CIT(A) should endeavor to dispose of the appeal within one year from the end of the financial year in which the appeal was filed, but this is advisory, not mandatory. Cases can sometimes take several years to conclude.
