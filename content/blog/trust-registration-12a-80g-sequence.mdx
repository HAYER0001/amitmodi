# Trust registration and 12A/80G: the sequence that avoids a re-application

To avoid re-application, first register your trust deed with the Sub-Registrar. Next, obtain your PAN and TAN. Then, simultaneously apply for provisional <Verify>12A</Verify> and <Verify>80G</Verify> registrations under <Verify>Form 10A</Verify>. Wait until operations commence, then apply for regular registration under <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> at least <Verify>6 months</Verify> prior to the provisional period's expiry.

For NGOs, trusts, and charitable institutions in India, securing income tax exemptions under Section <Verify>12A</Verify> and offering donation deductions under Section <Verify>80G</Verify> of the <Verify>Income Tax Act, 1961</Verify> are critical milestones. However, the sequence in which you execute the formation and tax registration is paramount. Getting the sequence wrong often leads to rejections, necessitating a complete re-application and delaying your philanthropic goals.

In this comprehensive guide, we will explore the precise roadmap to seamlessly register your trust and secure the necessary tax registrations without having to redo the process. This guide is crafted to provide you with the most thorough understanding of each step, the timelines involved, and the pitfalls to avoid.

## Why is the sequence of registration so critical?

When you establish a charitable trust, society, or Section 8 company, the primary goal is often to begin charitable work immediately and solicit donations. To incentivize these donations, the government provides tax reliefs to the donors. It also provides exemptions to the trust's own income so that the maximum amount of funds can be applied to charitable objectives.

If you apply for these exemptions out of sequence, the Income Tax Department may reject your application due to missing prerequisites or timeline violations. 

The <Verify>Finance Act, 2020</Verify> entirely overhauled the registration process, replacing the older perpetual registrations with a two-tier system: provisional registration and regular registration. This shift made the timeline and sequence even more delicate. A misstep might not only mean a rejected application but could also trigger tax liabilities on your corpus funds under <Verify>Section 115TD</Verify> at the maximum marginal rate of <Verify>34.944%</Verify>. 

Moreover, a rejection becomes a permanent mark on your entity's record, which often invites stricter scrutiny in any future applications. By following the precise sequence outlined below, you ensure a smooth, penalty-free path to operational readiness.

## What is the first step in forming a trust?

The very first step is drafting and executing the Trust Deed. The trust deed is the foundational document that outlines the objectives, rules, and operations of the trust. It acts as the constitution of your charitable organization.

### Drafting the Trust Deed

The drafting process requires meticulous attention to detail. The deed must clearly state:
- The name of the trust.
- The principal office address.
- The names, addresses, and occupations of the settlor and the trustees.
- The primary charitable objectives (which must align with the definition of charitable purpose under Section <Verify>2(15)</Verify> of the Income Tax Act).
- The rules governing the appointment, removal, and succession of trustees.
- Clauses prohibiting the distribution of profits or assets to the trustees.
- A dissolution clause stating that upon dissolution, the net assets will be transferred to another trust with similar objectives and valid <Verify>12A</Verify> registration.

### Registering the Deed

Once the deed is drafted, it must be registered with the jurisdictional Sub-Registrar. This requires the physical presence of the settlor (founder) and at least <Verify>2 witnesses</Verify>. 

You must pay stamp duty, which varies from state to state. For instance, in some states, the stamp duty for a charitable trust is a flat fee of around <Verify>₹500</Verify>, while in others, it is a percentage (e.g., <Verify>2% to 5%</Verify>) of the trust's movable or immovable property. Registration not only gives legal validity to the trust but is also a mandatory prerequisite for PAN application and subsequent tax exemptions.

## When should you apply for the PAN and TAN?

As soon as the trust deed is registered and you receive the registered copy from the Sub-Registrar, the next immediate step is to apply for a Permanent Account Number (PAN) and a Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number (TAN).

You cannot apply for any income tax exemptions without a valid PAN in the name of the trust. Ensure that the name on the PAN application perfectly matches the name on the registered trust deed. Even a minor discrepancy, such as a missing space or a different spelling, can cause massive delays in the subsequent exemption applications, as the automated systems will flag the mismatch.

The PAN application is made in <Verify>Form 49A</Verify>. Once the PAN is allotted, you must also apply for a TAN in <Verify>Form 49B</Verify> if your trust intends to deduct TDS on salaries, rent, professional fees, or payments to contractors. Even small NGOs often end up needing a TAN because they hire staff or rent office space.

## How does the provisional registration for 12A and 80G work?

Under the new regime effective from <Verify>April 1, 2021</Verify>, all newly established trusts must first apply for "Provisional Registration" before they commence their charitable activities. This was introduced to streamline the initial setup phase, allowing NGOs to start raising funds and operating without waiting months for intense scrutiny.

### Filing Form 10A

The application for provisional registration under both Section <Verify>12A</Verify> (for the trust's own income tax exemption) and Section <Verify>80G</Verify> (to allow donors to claim deductions) is filed electronically using <Verify>Form 10A</Verify>. 

You must file <Verify>Form 10A</Verify> at least <Verify>1 month</Verify> before the commencement of the previous year relevant to the assessment year for which the registration is sought. 

The provisional registration is granted in an automated manner, without any detailed inquiry into the activities of the trust, since the trust is assumed to have not yet commenced its activities. The system relies on the documents uploaded and the declarations made. 

The provisional registration is valid for a maximum period of <Verify>3 years</Verify> from the assessment year from which the registration is sought.

### Simultaneous vs. Sequential filing of 12A and 80G

A common question is whether to apply for <Verify>12A</Verify> and <Verify>80G</Verify> simultaneously. 

Yes, it is highly recommended to apply for both provisional <Verify>12A</Verify> and provisional <Verify>80G</Verify> at the same time using <Verify>Form 10A</Verify>. The Income Tax portal allows for simultaneous applications, saving time and administrative effort. Doing this sequentially often confuses the assessing officers and delays the process. Since the provisional stage is largely automated, batching them together is the most efficient strategy.

## When and how do you transition to regular registration?

Provisional registration is only temporary. It acts as a bridge. Once your trust actually commences its charitable activities, you must transition to a regular registration. 

This transition involves filing <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify>. The timing here is the most crucial part of the sequence. If you miss this deadline, your provisional registration lapses, your trust becomes taxable, and you will have to re-apply from scratch, potentially facing severe penalties.

### The strict timeline for Form 10AB

You must file <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> for regular registration within the following timelines, whichever is earlier:
1. Within <Verify>6 months</Verify> from the date of commencement of charitable activities.
2. At least <Verify>6 months</Verify> prior to the expiry of the period of the provisional registration (which is <Verify>3 years</Verify>).

Defining the "commencement of charitable activities" can sometimes be subjective. Generally, the moment you begin applying funds towards the objectives stated in your trust deed—such as organizing a medical camp, distributing educational materials, or paying salaries to staff engaged in charitable work—the clock starts ticking.

### The danger of missing the deadline

If you fail to file <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> within the stipulated time, the trust loses its exemption status. Furthermore, the trust may be subjected to exit tax under <Verify>Section 115TD</Verify>.

Exit tax is a draconian provision where the "accreted income" (which is essentially the fair market value of total assets minus total liabilities) is taxed at the maximum marginal rate of <Verify>34.944%</Verify>. Imagine losing over a third of your trust's assets simply because you missed a filing deadline by a few days!

This is why maintaining meticulous books of accounts, tracking every expense, and reconciling them properly is essential. You need precise dates of when activities commenced. ## What documents are required for these applications?

A successful application sequence relies heavily on having the correct, perfectly formatted documents ready at each stage. Missing, incomplete, or blurry documents are the primary reason for rejections and demands for re-application.

Here is a structured list of documents typically required during the various registration phases:

| Registration Phase | Form Used | Key Documents Required for Successful Filing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Trust Registration** | Varies by State | Draft Trust Deed, ID/Address Proof (Aadhaar/Passport) of Settlor & Trustees, Property Documents (if any immovable property is transferred), Passport-size Photographs, Utility bills for address proof. |
| **PAN Application** | <Verify>Form 49A</Verify> | Registered Trust Deed, Certificate of Registration (if issued by the Sub-Registrar), Board Resolution authorizing a trustee. |
| **Provisional 12A/80G** | <Verify>Form 10A</Verify> | Registered Trust Deed, PAN Card, Note on intended activities, digital signature of the authorized trustee. |
| **Regular 12A/80G** | <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> | Registered Trust Deed, PAN Card, Detailed note on actual activities conducted, Audited Financial Statements, Audit Reports in <Verify>Form 10B/10BB</Verify>, Bank Statements for the last <Verify>3 years</Verify> (or since inception), Details of CSR funds (if any), Details of major donors. |

## How does the scrutiny process work for Form 10AB?

Unlike the provisional registration via <Verify>Form 10A</Verify>, which is largely automated and trust-based, the application for regular registration via <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> involves a detailed, rigorous scrutiny by the Commissioner of Income Tax (Exemption) [CIT(E)].

The CIT(E) will examine several aspects before granting regular registration:
- **The genuineness of the trust's activities**: Are the activities actually charitable? Do they align with the trust deed? Are they benefiting the public at large rather than a specific religious community or caste (which would violate Section <Verify>13</Verify>)?
- **Compliance with other laws**: Has the trust complied with the requirements of any other law that is material for achieving its objectives? For example, if it runs a school, does it have the necessary state education board approvals?
- **Application of funds**: Have the funds been applied solely for charitable purposes? Have there been any unreasonable benefits provided to the trustees or their relatives (prohibited under Section <Verify>13</Verify>)?

The CIT(E) may issue notices requesting additional documents, photographs of events, or physical inspections of the premises. It is imperative to respond to these notices comprehensively and within the specified deadlines. 

The CIT(E) must pass an order in writing granting or rejecting the regular registration within <Verify>6 months</Verify> from the end of the month in which the <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> application was received.

If the CIT(E) is satisfied, they will grant regular registration, which is valid for a period of <Verify>5 years</Verify>. After <Verify>5 years</Verify>, the trust must apply for renewal using <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> at least <Verify>6 months</Verify> before the expiry of the 5-year period.

## Can you apply for 80G without 12A?

This is a frequent point of confusion. Technically, <Verify>Section 80G</Verify> provides tax deductions to the donors who contribute to your trust, while <Verify>Section 12A</Verify> provides income tax exemptions to the trust's own income. 

While the Income Tax Act does not explicitly, in plain text, state that <Verify>12A</Verify> is an absolute pre-requisite for <Verify>80G</Verify>, in practice, the Income Tax Department will almost never grant an <Verify>80G</Verify> certificate unless the trust already possesses or has simultaneously applied for <Verify>12A</Verify> registration. 

The rationale is logical: if the trust's own income is not exempt under <Verify>12A</Verify>, it is liable to pay tax on the donations it receives. The government does not want to grant tax deductions to donors if the receiving entity itself is going to be taxed on those funds as regular commercial income. Therefore, the unbreakable sequence mandates applying for <Verify>12A</Verify> before or exactly alongside <Verify>80G</Verify>.

## What happens if your application is rejected?

If your application in <Verify>Form 10A</Verify> or <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> is rejected due to a sequencing error, incomplete documents, or failure to respond to notices from the CIT(E), you will face significant administrative and financial hurdles.

Firstly, you will have to file a fresh application. This resets the clock. During the intervening period, any donations or income received will be treated as normal income and taxed at the applicable rates. Donors will not be able to claim <Verify>80G</Verify> deductions, which can severely impact your fundraising efforts and damage your reputation. 

In some cases, if the rejection occurs during the transition from provisional to regular registration, the draconian provisions of <Verify>Section 115TD</Verify> (tax on accreted income) may be invoked, as the department may argue that the trust has lost its charitable status. 

This highlights why getting the sequence and documentation right the first time is not just a matter of convenience, but a matter of financial survival for the NGO.

## A summary of the perfect sequence

To summarize and reinforce the roadmap we have discussed, here is the flawless sequence to follow to ensure your registrations are granted smoothly:

1. **Draft and execute the Trust Deed**: Ensure absolute clarity on charitable objectives and include all mandatory statutory clauses.
2. **Register the Deed**: Complete the registration process at the local Sub-Registrar's office.
3. **Obtain PAN and TAN**: Apply immediately post-registration, ensuring the name matches the deed perfectly.
4. **Open a Bank Account**: Essential for receiving the initial corpus and preparing for future donations.
5. **Apply for Provisional 12A and 80G**: File <Verify>Form 10A</Verify> simultaneously before commencing any charitable activities.
6. **Commence Charitable Activities**: Begin executing the objectives outlined in the deed and meticulously record the date of commencement.
7. **Apply for Regular 12A and 80G**: File <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> within <Verify>6 months</Verify> of commencing activities or <Verify>6 months</Verify> prior to the expiry of the 3-year provisional period, whichever is earlier.
8. **Maintain Compliance**: Maintain audited accounts, file annual ITRs (<Verify>ITR-7</Verify>), and renew regular registrations every <Verify>5 years</Verify>.

## FAQs

### What is the penalty if I miss the Form 10AB deadline?
If you fail to file <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> within the specified timeframe, the trust loses its exemption. More severely, it may be subject to exit tax under <Verify>Section 115TD</Verify>, taxing the accreted income (net assets) at the maximum marginal rate of <Verify>34.944%</Verify>.

### How long is the provisional registration valid?
Provisional registration granted under <Verify>Form 10A</Verify> is valid for a maximum period of <Verify>3 years</Verify> from the assessment year from which the registration is sought.

### Can I apply for regular registration directly without provisional registration?
No. Under the amended provisions effective <Verify>April 1, 2021</Verify>, all new trusts must first obtain provisional registration using <Verify>Form 10A</Verify>. Direct regular registration is no longer permitted for newly formed entities, even if they have already commenced activities.

### Is the scrutiny for Form 10A and Form 10AB the same?
No. <Verify>Form 10A</Verify> (provisional) is an automated process with minimal scrutiny, granted largely on the assumption that activities haven't commenced. <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> (regular) involves intense, manual scrutiny by the CIT(E) regarding the genuineness of actual activities and strict compliance with all relevant laws.

### How much time does the CIT(E) take to process Form 10AB?
The CIT(E) is mandated by law to pass an order granting or rejecting the regular registration within <Verify>6 months</Verify> from the end of the month in which the <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> application was filed.

### Do I need to renew the regular registration?
Yes, regular registration is valid for a maximum period of <Verify>5 years</Verify>. You must apply for renewal using <Verify>Form 10AB</Verify> at least <Verify>6 months</Verify> before the expiry of this 5-year period to ensure continuous exemption.
