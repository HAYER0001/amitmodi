## What is entity formation and which structure is right for my business?

Entity formation is the legal process of structuring a business as a Private Limited Company, Limited Liability Partnership, or traditional Partnership. Proper incorporation limits personal liability, defines ownership rights, ensures statutory compliance with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, and provides a formal structure for raising capital.

## Who needs this

Entity formation is not merely an optional administrative task; it is a fundamental statutory requirement for entrepreneurs, startups, joint ventures, and foreign subsidiaries aiming to conduct business systematically in India. Any group of individuals or corporate bodies that wish to pool resources, limit their personal liability, and create a distinct legal entity separate from its owners requires proper entity formation. Specifically, businesses seeking to raise equity funding from venture capitalists or angel investors must typically incorporate as a Private Limited Company, as investors demand the governance structures and equity allocation mechanisms that this specific entity type provides. Furthermore, businesses aiming to scale operations, hire employees under a formal corporate banner, bid for government tenders, or enter into significant commercial contracts will find that most counterparties require a formalized corporate entity. Even small-scale businesses that carry high operational risks, such as manufacturing or specialized consulting, need entity formation to shield personal assets from potential corporate debts and liabilities.

## What the process actually involves

The process of entity formation, particularly for a Private Limited Company or Limited Liability Partnership (LLP), is a multi-step statutory procedure governed primarily by the Companies Act, 2013 <!-- VERIFY --> and the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 <!-- VERIFY -->. It involves rigorous interaction with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) portal, drafting of constitutive documents, and strict adherence to regulatory timelines.

**Step 1: Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) Acquisition**
The foundational step is obtaining a Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) <!-- VERIFY --> for all proposed directors and designated partners. This requires the submission of self-attested identity and address proofs, such as a PAN card and Aadhaar card, to a Certifying Authority (CA) authorized by the Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA). The applicant must undergo a mandatory video verification process and mobile OTP authentication. Without a valid DSC, no forms can be filed on the MCA portal.

**Step 2: Director Identification Number (DIN) / Designated Partner Identification Number (DPIN)**
While previously a separate process, the application for a DIN is now integrated into the incorporation form (SPICe+ Part B) <!-- VERIFY --> for up to three new directors <!-- VERIFY -->. However, if an individual already holds a DIN, they must use the existing one. The DIN is a unique lifetime identifier assigned to directors.

**Step 3: Name Reservation (SPICe+ Part A / RUN-LLP)**
The proposed name must be reserved through the SPICe+ Part A form <!-- VERIFY --> for companies or RUN-LLP form <!-- VERIFY --> for LLPs. The name must adhere strictly to the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 <!-- VERIFY -->. It cannot be identical or too similar to an existing company name, LLP name, or a registered trademark under the Trade Marks Act, 1999 <!-- VERIFY -->. The MCA officer conducts a manual and algorithmic check. If approved, the name is reserved for 20 days <!-- VERIFY --> (for companies) or 90 days <!-- VERIFY --> (for LLPs).

**Step 4: Drafting of Constitutive Documents (MOA and AOA)**
For a company, the Memorandum of Association (MOA) and Articles of Association (AOA) must be drafted. The MOA defines the company's objects (what it can do), while the AOA dictates its internal regulations. These are typically filed as e-MOA (INC-33) <!-- VERIFY --> and e-AOA (INC-34) <!-- VERIFY -->. For an LLP, an LLP Agreement must be drafted, which is filed post-incorporation within 30 days <!-- VERIFY --> in Form 3 <!-- VERIFY -->.

**Step 5: Filing the Incorporation Forms**
The core filing involves the SPICe+ Part B form <!-- VERIFY -->, which consolidates applications for incorporation, DIN allotment, PAN (Permanent Account Number), TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number), EPFO (Employees' Provident Fund Organization) registration <!-- VERIFY -->, ESIC (Employees' State Insurance Corporation) registration <!-- VERIFY -->, Profession Tax (in specific states like Maharashtra) <!-- VERIFY -->, and Bank Account opening. This form must be accompanied by the AGILE-PRO-S form <!-- VERIFY --> and INC-9 (Declaration by Subscribers and First Directors) <!-- VERIFY -->.

**Step 6: Processing by the Central Registration Centre (CRC)**
Once filed and the prescribed stamp duty is paid, the forms are processed by the Central Registration Centre (CRC). A Registrar of Companies (ROC) officer scrutinizes the documents. If discrepancies are found, the officer issues a "Resubmission" remark, granting typically 15 days <!-- VERIFY --> to rectify the errors.

**Step 7: Certificate of Incorporation (COI)**
If the CRC officer finds the application in order, they issue the Certificate of Incorporation (COI) dynamically, which includes the Corporate Identification Number (CIN) <!-- VERIFY -->, PAN, and TAN. The entity is now legally born.

## Documents you will need

The documentation requirements are exhaustive and must be impeccably accurate. Any mismatch can lead to outright rejection or requests for resubmission.

**For Proposed Directors / Promoters:**
- Permanent Account Number (PAN) Card (Mandatory for Indian nationals)
- Aadhaar Card / Voter ID / Passport / Driving License (as Identity Proof)
- Recent Bank Statement / Utility Bill (Electricity, Telephone, Mobile) not older than 2 months <!-- VERIFY --> (as Address Proof)
- Passport-sized photographs

**For the Registered Office:**
- Proof of ownership (Conveyance deed / Sale deed) or Rent Agreement / Lease Deed with the landlord.
- No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the owner of the premises stating that the company can use the premises as its registered office.
- Latest Utility Bill (Electricity, Gas, Telephone) in the name of the owner, not older than 2 months <!-- VERIFY -->.

## How long it takes and what can delay it

Under ideal circumstances, if all documents are perfect and the proposed name is unique, entity formation can be completed in 7 to 10 working days <!-- VERIFY -->. However, this is heavily contingent on the absence of failure modes.

**Failure Modes and Delays:**
1. **Name Rejection due to Trademark Infringement:** If the proposed name phonetically or visually resembles a registered trademark under Class 1 to 45 <!-- VERIFY -->, the CRC officer will reject it. This resets the process and delays it by 3-5 days <!-- VERIFY -->.
2. **Address Proof Mismatch:** If the address on the bank statement does not exactly match the address on the Aadhaar card (e.g., missing street name or different pin code), the DSC or DIN application will face resubmission.
3. **Utility Bill Older than Prescribed Limit:** Submitting a registered office utility bill that is 61 days old instead of the mandated maximum of 60 days <!-- VERIFY --> will result in an automatic resubmission remark from the MCA.
4. **Signatures Not Matching:** If the physical signature on the INC-9 declaration or NOC does not match the PAN database signature, the application is often flagged for manual review and subsequent resubmission.
5. **Vague Main Objects in MOA:** If the objects stated in the MOA (SPICe+ Part A) are too broad, ambiguous, or cover multiple unrelated industries (e.g., software development and agricultural farming), the ROC officer may demand clarification, delaying the process by 7 days <!-- VERIFY -->.
6. **Director Disqualification Check:** The portal automatically checks if a proposed director is disqualified under Section 164 of the Companies Act, 2013 <!-- VERIFY --> (e.g., due to non-filing of returns in another company). If true, the incorporation cannot proceed until the disqualification is removed.
7. **Foreign National Documentation:** If a subscriber is a foreign national, their documents must be notarized and apostilled as per the Hague Convention <!-- VERIFY -->, or consularized. Any error in this apostille process can cause weeks of delays.
8. **Bank Account API Failures:** The SPICe+ form integrates with banking APIs for automatic account opening. Sometimes, backend server timeouts between MCA and the chosen bank can delay the final issuance of the COI by 24-48 hours <!-- VERIFY -->.
9. **Stamp Duty Payment Gateway Issues:** The MCA21 portal occasionally faces payment gateway timeouts. If the stamp duty payment is debited but the SRN (Service Request Number) status is not updated to 'Paid', a ticket must be raised, taking 2-3 days <!-- VERIFY --> to resolve.
10. **Incomplete NOC:** Providing a generic rent agreement without a specific, signed NOC from the actual owner listed on the utility bill will lead to a guaranteed resubmission.

## What it costs

The cost of entity formation comprises professional fees and statutory government fees, which vary based on the authorized capital and state of registration.

- **Government Fees:** For a Private Limited Company with an authorized capital up to ₹15 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY -->, the MCA filing fee is generally NIL <!-- VERIFY -->. However, stamp duty is applicable and varies significantly by state. For example, stamp duty in Madhya Pradesh or Punjab is notably higher than in Delhi or Maharashtra.
- **PAN & TAN Fees:** Approximately ₹131 <!-- VERIFY --> and ₹65 <!-- VERIFY --> respectively.
- **DSC Procurement:** Roughly ₹1,500 to ₹2,500 <!-- VERIFY --> per director, depending on the vendor and validity (usually 2 years <!-- VERIFY -->).
- **Professional Fees:** Chartered Accountants, Company Secretaries, or legal professionals charge anywhere from ₹10,000 to ₹30,000+ <!-- VERIFY --> depending on the complexity, consulting required, and drafting of specialized clauses in the AOA.

## What happens if you do not do this

If you operate a business without formal entity formation (e.g., operating as an unregistered sole proprietorship or unregistered partnership):
- **Unlimited Personal Liability:** If the business incurs massive debts or faces a substantial lawsuit, creditors can legally seize your personal assets, including your home and personal bank accounts, to recover dues.
- **Inability to Raise Capital:** Institutional investors, Venture Capitalists (VCs), and Private Equity (PE) firms are statutorily and constitutionally restricted from investing in sole proprietorships or unregistered partnerships.
- **Loss of Tax Benefits:** You miss out on the reduced corporate tax rates. For instance, newly incorporated domestic manufacturing companies can avail a concessional tax rate of 15% under Section 115BAB of the Income Tax Act <!-- VERIFY -->, a benefit unavailable to proprietorships.
- **Tender Ineligibility:** Government and large corporate tenders almost universally mandate that bidders be registered companies or LLPs with a minimum vintage and formalized audit history.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing a Generic Name:** Selecting a name like "Global Tech Solutions" without checking the trademark registry. The MCA will reject it, wasting time and the ₹1,000 <!-- VERIFY --> SPICe+ Part A fee.
- **Ignoring AOA Clauses:** Using a standardized, copy-pasted Articles of Association (AOA) instead of customizing it to include drag-along, tag-along rights, or anti-dilution clauses if multiple founders are involved.
- **Inadequate Authorized Capital:** Incorporating with the bare minimum authorized capital of ₹1,00,000 <!-- VERIFY --> and subsequently needing to raise capital immediately, which then requires filing form SH-7 <!-- VERIFY -->, passing resolutions, and paying additional stamp duty and ROC fees.
- **Mixing Personal and Business Expenses Prior to Incorporation:** Promoters often spend personal money on business assets before the company exists without proper pre-incorporation contracts, making it complex to account for these as capital or loans later.
- **Delaying Post-Incorporation Compliances:** Failing to deposit the subscription money into the new company bank account and failing to file Form INC-20A (Declaration of Commencement of Business) <!-- VERIFY --> within 180 days <!-- VERIFY -->. This specific failure leads to a penalty of ₹50,000 <!-- VERIFY --> on the company and ₹1,000 per day <!-- VERIFY --> on directors, and the ROC can initiate strike-off proceedings.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a Private Limited Company be formed with just one person?**
No, a standard Private Limited Company requires a minimum of two directors and two shareholders <!-- VERIFY -->. However, a single individual can incorporate a One Person Company (OPC) under Section 2(62) of the Companies Act, 2013 <!-- VERIFY -->.

**Do I need a commercial office space to incorporate?**
No, a residential address can be used as the registered office of the company, provided you have a valid utility bill in the owner's name and a signed No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the owner.

**Is it mandatory for all directors to be Indian residents?**
No, foreign nationals can be directors. However, Section 149(3) of the Companies Act, 2013 <!-- VERIFY --> mandates that every company must have at least one director who has stayed in India for a total period of not less than 182 days <!-- VERIFY --> during the financial year.

**What is the difference between Authorized Capital and Paid-up Capital?**
Authorized Capital is the maximum amount of share capital a company is legally authorized to issue to shareholders, as stated in its MOA. Paid-up Capital is the actual amount of money the shareholders have paid to the company for the shares issued to them.

**Can we change the company name later?**
Yes, a company name can be changed post-incorporation by passing a Special Resolution <!-- VERIFY --> in a general meeting, obtaining approval from the Central Government (delegated to the ROC) via Form INC-24 <!-- VERIFY -->, and paying the requisite fees.
