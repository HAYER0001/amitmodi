## What is a PAN Card and why is it mandatory?
Permanent Account Number (PAN) is a mandatory ten-character alphanumeric identifier issued by the Indian Income Tax Department. It is legally required for individuals, corporate entities, and partnerships to file income tax returns, open bank accounts, receive taxable income, and conduct high-value financial transactions exceeding statutory limits within India.

## Who needs this

The requirement for a Permanent Account Number extends across virtually all economic strata and entity types operating within or deriving income from India. The Income Tax Act of 1961 explicitly mandates the procurement of a PAN for the following categories:

1. **Individuals with Taxable Income:** Any person whose total gross income exceeds the basic exemption limit (₹2.5 Lakhs for individuals under 60 years under the old tax regime, ₹3 Lakhs under the new regime <!-- VERIFY -->) is statutorily obligated to obtain a PAN.
2. **Business Owners and Professionals:** Every individual carrying on any business or profession whose total sales, turnover, or gross receipts are or are likely to exceed ₹5,00,000 <!-- VERIFY --> in any previous year must apply for a PAN.
3. **Importers and Exporters:** Any entity intending to import or export goods must obtain an Importer Exporter Code (IEC), which intrinsically requires a valid PAN.
4. **Corporate Entities and Trusts:** All registered companies (Private Limited, Public Limited, One Person Companies), Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs), Partnership Firms, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), Association of Persons (AOPs), Body of Individuals (BOIs), and Trusts must possess a PAN irrespective of their income or turnover.
5. **Foreign Nationals and Entities:** Non-resident individuals and foreign companies generating any taxable income within the jurisdiction of India must acquire a PAN (using Form 49AA <!-- VERIFY -->).
6. **Financial Transaction Participants:** Even individuals falling below taxable limits are required to quote a PAN for specific high-value transactions, such as purchasing motor vehicles, depositing cash exceeding ₹50,000 <!-- VERIFY --> in a single day at a banking company, or purchasing mutual funds exceeding ₹50,000 <!-- VERIFY -->.

## What the process actually involves

The procedure for obtaining a PAN is fundamentally divided into application preparation, biometric or demographic authentication, processing by government-appointed portals (Protean/NSDL or UTIITSL), and final issuance by the Income Tax Department.

### Step 1: Form Selection and Data Entry
The applicant must first determine the appropriate form. Form 49A is utilized for Indian citizens, Indian companies, and entities incorporated within India. Form 49AA is exclusively for foreign citizens and entities incorporated outside India. The data entry phase requires meticulous transcription of the applicant's name exactly as it appears on the primary identity document (typically Aadhaar for individuals).

### Step 2: Authentication and e-KYC (For Individuals)
The most streamlined method utilizes Aadhaar-based e-KYC. The portal fetches demographic details directly from the UIDAI database. The applicant receives a Time-Based One-Time Password (OTP) on the mobile number linked to their Aadhaar. If the Aadhaar is not linked to a mobile number, the applicant must utilize biometric authentication at an authorized PAN center or submit physical documents.

### Step 3: Document Upload and E-Sign (For Non-e-KYC Routes)
If e-KYC is not utilized, the applicant must upload scanned copies of their Proof of Identity (POI), Proof of Address (POA), and Proof of Date of Birth (PODB). Once uploaded, the application must be digitally signed using a Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) <!-- VERIFY --> (mandatory for companies and LLPs) or e-Signed using Aadhaar OTP.

### Step 4: Physical Submission (If Applicable)
If neither e-KYC nor e-Sign is viable, the applicant must print the filled application form, affix two recent passport-sized photographs, sign across the photograph (on the left) and in the designated box (on the right), and courier the physical docket along with self-attested copies of POI, POA, and PODB to the Protean or UTIITSL processing center.

### Step 5: Processing and Allotment
The processing center verifies the submitted data against the supporting documents. For individuals, a demographic match against the Income Tax Department's database ensures no duplicate PAN exists. Upon successful verification, an electronic PAN (e-PAN) is dispatched to the registered email address in PDF format, secured by a password (typically the Date of Birth in DDMMYYYY format <!-- VERIFY -->).

### Step 6: Physical Card Dispatch
Subsequently, the physical, laminated plastic PAN card is printed and dispatched via Speed Post to the communication address provided in the application.

## Documents you will need

The documentary requirements are strictly regulated under Rule 114 of the Income Tax Rules, 1962 <!-- VERIFY -->. The documents must clearly substantiate Identity, Address, and Date of Birth.

**For Individual Indian Citizens (Any ONE from each category):**
*   **Proof of Identity (POI):** Aadhaar Card issued by UIDAI, Elector's Photo Identity Card (Voter ID), Driving License, Passport, Ration Card having photograph of the applicant.
*   **Proof of Address (POA):** Aadhaar Card, Elector's Photo Identity Card, Driving License, Passport, Post Office passbook having address of the applicant, Latest property tax assessment order, Domicile certificate issued by the Government, Allotment letter of accommodation issued by the Central/State Government of not more than 3 years old <!-- VERIFY -->, Property Registration Document.
*   **Proof of Date of Birth (PODB):** Birth Certificate issued by the Municipal Authority or any office authorized to issue Birth and Death Certificate by the Registrar of Birth and Deaths, Pension payment order, Marriage certificate issued by Registrar of Marriages, Matriculation certificate or mark sheet of recognized board.

**For Hindu Undivided Families (HUF):**
*   An affidavit by the Karta of the HUF stating the name, father's name, and address of all the coparceners on the date of application.
*   Copy of document applicable to the Karta as POI, POA, and PODB.

**For Companies Registered in India:**
*   Copy of the Certificate of Registration issued by the Registrar of Companies (ROC).

**For Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs) and Partnership Firms:**
*   Copy of Certificate of Registration issued by the Registrar of LLPs or Registrar of Firms, OR Copy of the Partnership Deed.

**For Trusts:**
*   Copy of Trust Deed OR Copy of Certificate of Registration Number issued by Charity Commissioner.

## How long it takes and what can delay it

Under ideal circumstances, an Aadhaar-based e-KYC application results in e-PAN generation within 10 to 48 hours <!-- VERIFY -->. Physical card delivery typically takes 7 to 15 working days <!-- VERIFY -->. However, numerous failure modes can severely delay the process, resulting in applications being put on hold indefinitely.

### Specific Failure Modes and Delays:

1.  **Aadhaar Demographic Mismatch (The most common delay):** The Income Tax portal mandates an exact character-by-character match between the name, date of birth, and gender on the PAN application and the UIDAI database. If an applicant's name has initials in Aadhaar but expanded in the PAN application, the e-KYC will outright fail, forcing a physical or document-upload route, extending timelines by 15-30 days <!-- VERIFY -->.
2.  **Signature Rejection (Physical Forms):** The Income Tax Department uses automated optical character recognition (OCR) and manual verification for signatures. If the signature breaches the boundaries of the designated box on the physical form, or if a stamp/seal obscures the signature for corporate applicants, the application is rejected.
3.  **Photograph Quality Deficiencies:** If the uploaded or affixed photograph has a dark background, shadows on the face, or the applicant is wearing tinted glasses, the image processing algorithm will flag it for manual review, often leading to a rejection and requiring a fresh submission.
4.  **Jurisdictional Assessing Officer (AO) Assignment Errors:** Every PAN must be mapped to a specific Assessing Officer code comprising Area Code, AO Type, Range Code, and AO Number. Incorrect selection based on geographical location or entity type will cause the backend system to route the application to the wrong jurisdictional desk, which will then bounce it back.
5.  **Obscure or Illegible Documentary Evidence:** If the uploaded scanned copies of POI/POA are blurry, cropped, or not self-attested (where required), the processing center will issue a defect notice. The applicant must then mail physical clarifications, adding 3-4 weeks <!-- VERIFY --> to the timeline.
6.  **Duplicate PAN Suspicions (System Generated):** If the applicant shares a very common name and date of birth with an existing PAN holder, the system may flag it as a potential duplicate. A manual officer review is triggered to ensure the applicant is not attempting to obtain multiple PANs (a severe offense under Section 272B <!-- VERIFY -->).
7.  **Address Proof Rejection for Corporate Entities:** Providing a residential address for a company's registered office without a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the property owner, or providing a lease agreement that has expired, will lead to immediate withholding of the allotment.
8.  **Communication Address Anomalies:** If the postal code does not match the state selected, or if the address is deemed 'incomplete' by the postal service database integration, the physical card dispatch is halted.

## What happens if you do not do this

Failure to obtain, quote, or link a PAN when mandated by the Income Tax Act invites severe statutory penalties, financial restrictions, and extreme scrutiny from the Revenue Department.

1.  **Statutory Penalty under Section 272B:** A direct penalty of ₹10,000 <!-- VERIFY --> can be levied by the Assessing Officer for failure to comply with the provisions of section 139A (which mandates obtaining a PAN).
2.  **Higher Rate of Tax Deduction at Source (TDS):** Under Section 206AA <!-- VERIFY --> of the Income Tax Act, if a payee fails to furnish their PAN to the deductor, tax shall be deducted at the higher of the following rates: the rate specified in the relevant provision, the rate in force, or a punitive rate of 20% <!-- VERIFY -->. For certain non-filers, this can even escalate under Section 206AB.
3.  **Inability to Open Bank Accounts or Demat Accounts:** RBI regulations strictly prohibit the opening of standard savings, current, or demat accounts without a valid PAN. You will be entirely locked out of the formal banking sector.
4.  **Restrictions on High-Value Transactions:** You cannot purchase or sell immovable property exceeding ₹10 Lakhs <!-- VERIFY -->, purchase four-wheeled vehicles, or book foreign travel exceeding ₹50,000 <!-- VERIFY --> without quoting a PAN. Attempts to bypass this using Form 60 are heavily scrutinized and often audited.
5.  **Rejection of Income Tax Returns:** It is technologically impossible to file an Income Tax Return or claim a refund of any excess tax paid without a valid, active PAN.
6.  **Aadhaar-PAN Linkage Default:** If an allotted PAN is not linked to Aadhaar by the mandated deadline (currently carrying a late fee of ₹1,000 <!-- VERIFY --> under Section 234H), the PAN becomes **inoperative**. An inoperative PAN is legally treated as if the person does not possess a PAN at all, triggering all the aforementioned TDS penalties and transaction blocks.

### Detailed Example of Non-Compliance Consequences
Consider an independent consultant who earns ₹8,00,000 from various corporate clients but neglects to apply for a PAN. When a client pays an invoice of ₹1,00,000, they are required to deduct TDS under section 194J at 10%. Because the consultant has no PAN, Section 206AA kicks in. The client is legally forced to deduct TDS at 20%. The consultant loses ₹20,000 to taxes instead of ₹10,000. Furthermore, because the consultant has no PAN, they cannot file a return to claim a refund of this ₹20,000. They have effectively lost this money permanently. Concurrently, if the Income Tax Department discovers this income stream through other intelligence, they can levy the ₹10,000 penalty under 272B for not holding a PAN, plus demand the tax due along with interest under sections 234A, 234B, and 234C.

## Common mistakes

*   **Using Nicknames or Unofficial Names:** Applicants often use shortened names (e.g., 'Ravi' instead of 'Ravindra'). The Income Tax database requires the full legal name exactly as it appears on primary identity documents.
*   **Applying for Multiple PANs:** Sometimes applicants lose their PAN card and, out of ignorance, apply for a new one instead of requesting a reprint. Holding more than one PAN is illegal and attracts a ₹10,000 penalty <!-- VERIFY -->.
*   **Incorrect Assessing Officer (AO) Code Selection:** Guessing the AO code instead of verifying it through the official NSDL/UTIITSL lookup tool causes the application to bounce between jurisdictions.
*   **Not Linking Aadhaar and PAN:** Many applicants obtain the PAN but fail to complete the linkage process on the e-Filing portal, rendering the PAN inoperative after the statutory deadline.
*   **Ignoring Defect Notices:** If an application is placed on hold due to a discrepancy, the processing center sends an email. Applicants frequently ignore these, assuming the process will eventually resolve itself, resulting in indefinite suspension.

### Detailed Example of a Common Mistake
A newly formed Private Limited Company needs a PAN to open a current account. The director fills out Form 49A. However, under the 'Name' section, they write "ABC Pvt. Ltd." instead of the exact legally incorporated name "ABC Technologies Private Limited" as shown on the Certificate of Incorporation. They upload the COI and submit. The automated processing system detects the discrepancy between the application string and the OCR-extracted string from the COI. The application is instantly put on hold with a defect notice. Because the director used a generic email address that they rarely check, they miss the defect notice. Three weeks later, the bank refuses to open the account, business operations are stalled, and they realize the application was never processed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a minor apply for a PAN card?**
Yes, minors can apply for a PAN card. However, the application must be signed by a Representative Assessee (parent or legal guardian). The minor's photograph does not appear on the PAN card; instead, it bears the word "MINOR" and the signature of the guardian. Once the minor attains majority (18 years), they must apply for an update to the PAN to include their own photograph and signature.

**Is it mandatory to link Aadhaar with PAN for companies?**
No, the Aadhaar-PAN linkage mandate applies strictly to individual taxpayers who are eligible to obtain an Aadhaar number. Companies, LLPs, Firms, and Trusts do not possess an Aadhaar and are therefore exempt from this specific linkage requirement.

**What should I do if I have been allotted two PANs by mistake?**
You must immediately surrender the duplicate/unused PAN to the Income Tax Department. This requires filing an application for cancellation using the 'Changes or Correction in existing PAN Data' form, specifying the PAN to be retained and the PAN(s) to be cancelled, and submitting it to your jurisdictional Assessing Officer physically or via the official portal to avoid the ₹10,000 penalty.
