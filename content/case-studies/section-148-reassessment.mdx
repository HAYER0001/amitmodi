## Situation
A mid-sized manufacturing firm in Pune received a notice under Section 148 of the Income Tax Act for a previous assessment year. The department alleged that an amount of ₹85 Lakhs had escaped assessment due to a discrepancy between the reported turnover and the deposits reflected in the company's bank accounts.

## Task
We needed to formulate a robust, evidence-backed defense to prove that all deposits were accounted for and derived from declared business receipts, thereby preventing the addition of the amount to their taxable income and the imposition of severe penalties.

## Action
1. **Fact Discovery:** We obtained the reasons recorded by the Assessing Officer for reopening the assessment.
2. **Reconciliation:** We mapped the bank statements line-by-line against the cash book, sales register, and previously filed ITR-6. We identified that the discrepancy arose from inter-bank transfers and a short-term unsecured loan which were mistakenly flagged as unexplained revenue.
3. **Submission Drafting:** We drafted a detailed factual and legal submission, attaching the reconciliation statement, loan confirmations, and relevant board resolutions.
4. **Representation:** We uploaded the complete response through the Faceless Assessment portal within the statutory 30-day window and filed the required revised computations.

## Result
The Assessing Officer accepted the submissions and dropped the proposed additions. The reassessment proceedings were concluded with no extra tax demand or penalty levied against the client.
